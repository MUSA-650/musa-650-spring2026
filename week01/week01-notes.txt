Week 01 Notes - MUSA 6500

- Determining what tool is best for the job
	- Use a Neural Network that takes a while and gets you 85% accuracy, or a Random Forest model that gets you to 75% and is simpler.
	- "Black Box" methods --> you don't understand why the model is predicting the way it is, you just know that the output is correct.
		- Neural Networks, until recently, were a prime example of this.
- Downsampling (making resolution coarser) is a filter --> it will filter out 'noise' present at finer resolutions
	- Coarser resolutions are available more frequently (satellites orbit faster), while finer resolutions are available less frequently
	- Overlaying coarse data (thermal anomaly, 300m) with fine data (burn index, 10m) can yield more useful results
		- Can help identify larger regions where fires might be present and then pinpoint specific locations.
- Biases in data
	- Up to 90% of research is focused on Asia, Europe, and North America
	- Global South is often excluded and models built on the Global North often predict worse
- Data Sources:
	- Mapbiomas
		- 30m resolution land cover imagery for all of Argentina from 1998
	- Sentinel-1 SAR
		- Flood mapping in Valencia
	- Sentinel-2 images and OpenStreetMap
		- Mapping urban green spaces in Latin American cities
	- Google Open Building Footprints
		- Used to train green space recognition models
		- Creating segmentation masks of building footprints using satellite imagery
		- Helpful for detecting and mapping informal settlements (Nissim's work in Argentina)
			- Census said 330 families, imagery detected almost 4.2k buildings
			- Pulling a dataset that's already been produced, instead of doing all the processing yourself
		- While errors might exist, often automated workflows actually have less errors than manual workflows
- Issues with deep learning for urban planning applications:
	- Ground truth data for validation is often lacking
	- Workflows are often too difficult to understand, computationally intensive, and costly for under-resourced cities
	- Bureaucracies tend to favor established workflows and actively thwart new approaches
Opportunities for deep learning in urban planning:
	- Tech is getting more accurate and less costly  --> more availability of data
	- Cloud computing advancements make large deep learning models and datasets more accessible
	- While cities need to be more open to remote sensing techniques
	- Researchers need to be more aware of local governance methods and policies to better accommodate
