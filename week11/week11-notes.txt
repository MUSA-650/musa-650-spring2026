Tristan Grupp Lecture: Field Boundary Mapping for Sustainable Supply Chains in South America
---------------------------------
- Work for the American Geophysical Union
- UNet model to identify agricultural boundaries

Motivation
- 90-99% of deforestation driven by agriculture, driven by 7 commodities (oil palm, soy, wood, cocoa, coffee, cattle, rubber)
	- 2000-2019, deforestation from soy doubled from 26.4MHa to 55.1MHa
	- To sell to EU, SA farmers have to prove that these crops did not contribute to deforestation

Fields of the World (FTW) Model --> Kerner et al 2025 "Fields of the World: A 
- Two windows A and B and semantic layer
	- A = growing season (greener)
	- B = harvested (browner)
	- Semantic = labelled boundaries
- 70,000 samples with 1.4 million fields, across 24 countries
	- Only 1.5% of training data from SA
	- 10m resolution training data
		- Being adapted to Planet Imagery for use in Africa, 3-5m resolution but lower pixel contrast
- Different types of fields
	- Catinga (rectangular), wetland/floodable field hybrids, radial fields w/village at center, mountain cultivation, etc.
- Data sharing stinginess
	- Investment in new training data with full-time hand annotators
	- Using ArcGIS Pro, drawing overall boundary of agricultural extent and clipping out individual fields from shape
- Sampling
	- Sampled 50 training chips within Sentinel-2 tiles with poor base model performance
	- Edited in small holder fields from existing data boundaries
	- Mato Grosso, rly difficult region to model
		- Softmax outputs used to identify regions of low confidence (output -1 to 1)
		- Burn scars from intentional fire clearing confused the model
		- Model was adapted to identify burning
		- Compared to high-quality land cover classification outputs "land cover discrepancy samples"
			- More samples in the south due to type of agriculture
	- Fine-tuning of model dragged down Pixel IOU and Recall
		- Addressed with "Catastrophic Forgetting"
		- Transfer learning employed, was incredibly crucial
			- Merging models from different locations actually improved results
- Estimating crop yield in Africa
	- Uses NDVI, atmospheric/soil moisture, time-varying precipitation, temperature, growing degree days
	- Signals are unique to each crop
	- Model can be used to determine whether other crops might be more suitable
